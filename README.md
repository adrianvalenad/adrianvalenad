## Latest: NAD supplement dosage, what the trials tested is not what you should take


Search for an NAD supplement dose and you will usually get one tidy number. In preparing the NAD+Nerd dosage explainer, I found the research is much less interchangeable than that. This post is about how to read study amounts, and it is not a recommendation to take any of them.

Nicotinamide riboside (NR) and nicotinamide mononucleotide (NMN) are NAD-related precursors, and an oral precursor trial is not a dose-finding study for an NAD+ injection. Even two oral products need the ingredient, formulation and serving size identified before their amounts can be compared. And the most important question is what an amount achieved: raising a blood marker, improving a metabolic measurement and relieving a symptom are separate results.

Consider the examples. Conze and colleagues randomized 140 otherwise healthy adults with overweight to placebo or 100, 300 or 1,000 mg of NR daily for eight weeks. Whole-blood NAD+ increased in the active groups, and the paper reported no significant adverse-event differences between groups. The study was funded by ChromaDex and disclosed author financial relationships. Its questions concerned metabolism and tolerability, not fatigue or lifespan.

Dollerup and colleagues studied 40 men with obesity and insulin resistance, without diabetes, in a placebo-controlled trial. NR participants received 1,000 mg twice daily for 12 weeks. The reported analysis did not find improvement in glucose tolerance, pancreatic hormone function or the measured incretin responses. That does not prove every possible use is ineffective, but it shows why lining products up from low to high milligrams is a poor substitute for looking at outcomes.

NMN gives a different example: a 10-week randomized trial in postmenopausal women with prediabetes tested 250 mg of NMN daily and reported improved muscle insulin sensitivity. That belongs to its population and intervention. It is not a head-to-head showing that one amount of NMN beats a particular NR amount.

What can a label settle? Start with the named ingredient and the amount per serving, then check how many capsules make that serving. A bottle with more milligrams does not reproduce a trial, particularly if the preparation, added ingredients or route differ. A hypothetical 600 mg blend that does not disclose each component cannot be matched to a 300 mg NR trial by dividing the headline number.

I keep the protocol and the decision apart. You can compare label clarity, supply period and purchase terms without picking a personal amount from a table. How a specific product fits your medical history and other medicines is a question for a qualified clinician or pharmacist.
